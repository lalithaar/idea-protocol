### **Idea Protocol — Project Overview**

The **Idea Protocol** is a simple, non-legal system for **sharing early ideas** with **clear, socially-respectful expectations**. It aims to remove the awkwardness and confusion that often accompanies informal idea-sharing, like worrying about intellectual property (IP) or feeling uncomfortable bringing up trust issues with mentors, professors, or collaborators. The goal is to offer an **easy, neutral way** to communicate intent without needing complex contracts (NDAs, etc.) or risk of misunderstanding.

---

### **Why It’s Needed:**

* **Traditional IP tools** (like NDAs) are great for **finished works**, but they’re heavy and uncomfortable for **early-stage ideas**—the very ideas that need **feedback**, **collaboration**, or **input** from others.

* Most **interpersonal relationships** (like between students, researchers, or colleagues) don't fit the formalities of legal protections, so these kinds of conversations can get **awkward** when it comes to ownership or intent.

* **Idea Protocol** aims to **set clear social and moral boundaries** for how ideas should be treated when shared, without the **heavy legal language**. It’s designed to **respect the creator’s intentions** without creating friction or distrust between parties.

---

### **The Basic Concept**:

* The **Idea Protocol** introduces **simple, short licenses** for idea-sharing that everyone can easily understand and use. These licenses **define the expectations** of how an idea can be discussed, reused, or developed.

* Think of it like adding a **license footer** to your ideas—just as you would with a code repository or a published article. It allows **participants in a conversation or project** to quickly establish how an idea can be treated.

* The **licenses** will be **standardized** so that anyone can instantly recognize what they mean, and both the originator and recipient of the idea are on the same page.

---

### **Key Benefits**:

1. **No Legal Complexity**: Avoids the need for long, formal legal documents. It uses plain language to define expectations.
2. **Clear Boundaries**: Prevents misunderstandings about how ideas can be used or shared.
3. **Respectful Exchange**: Encourages **open discussion** without the fear of idea theft or misuse.
4. **Easy to Implement**: Can be added as a simple footer or a note to any document, email, or project.
5. **For Everyone**: Whether you're a student, researcher, or startup founder, it applies to **any context** where you're sharing early ideas.

---

### **License Codes & Descriptions:**

The licenses will be **short, standardized**, and easy to reference. The structure will follow a consistent pattern, with each license code representing a **clear intent**:

* **IP-R** — *Review*: For **private feedback** or review. Others may discuss the idea but cannot reuse or share without explicit consent.
* **IP-C** — *Commons*: For **open discussion** or public sharing. The idea can be used freely, but **credit** is expected.
* **IP-D** — *Development*: For **collaborative projects** or group efforts. Co-ownership is assumed unless otherwise specified.
* **IP-O** — *Open*: For **public release** and the ability to create derivative works, with **attribution** required.
* **IP-P** — *Private*: For **private discussions only**. The idea cannot be shared or reused outside the defined group without consent.

---

### **General Template for Each License**:

The license for each idea will include a brief description and a clear breakdown of **what’s allowed** and **what’s not**. For example:

* **Purpose**: A simple statement about the license's intent.
* **Permissions**: What others can do with the idea (e.g., discuss, collaborate, share).
* **Restrictions**: What others **cannot** do without permission (e.g., reuse, share publicly).
* **Attribution**: How credit should be given.
* **Disclaimer**: A neutral note clarifying that the license is not a formal contract, but rather a **social and moral framework** for idea-sharing.

---

### **Example License Wording**:

* **IP-R — Review**:
  *“This idea is shared under the **Idea Protocol — IP-R**. Discussion and feedback are welcome; reuse requires explicit consent.”*

* **IP-C — Commons**:
  *“This idea is shared under the **Idea Protocol — IP-C**. Feel free to discuss, share, and reuse with proper credit.”*

* **IP-D — Development**:
  *“This project is shared under the **Idea Protocol — IP-D**. All contributors co-own the idea unless otherwise specified.”*

* **IP-O — Open**:
  *“This idea is shared under the **Idea Protocol — IP-O**. Feel free to use, modify, and build upon with proper attribution.”*

* **IP-P — Private**:
  *“This idea is shared under the **Idea Protocol — IP-P**. It is for private discussion only and should not be reused or shared without consent.”*

---

### **How to Use It**:

1. **Choose a License**: Decide which license best suits your idea-sharing situation (Review, Commons, Development, Open, Private).
2. **Add It**: Use the relevant license code as a simple footer or statement in emails, project notes, or documents.
   Example: *“This idea is shared under the **Idea Protocol — IP-R**.”*
3. **Set Expectations**: The license lets everyone know how the idea can be used and what to expect from the conversation or collaboration.
